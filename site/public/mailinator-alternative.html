Mailinator alternative: private inboxes your AI agent creates, reads and replies from

Mailinator is a receive-only email and SMS testing service: @mailinator.com inboxes are public, readable by anyone and emptied after a few hours, and private domains with API access come with the free Verified Pro plan or paid plans from $79 a month billed yearly (as of 7 November 2026). AgentFirstStack Mail gives your agent private inboxes it creates itself in one request, codes only from the sender it names, replies, and forwarding to you. Mailinator is the better choice for testing your own app's email at volume, SMS and authenticator codes, and routing rules.

Updated 7 November 2026

What Mailinator is

Mailinator, from Manybrain, describes itself as "more than a temporary email address": QA teams and developers use it to test sign-ups, password resets and 2FA codes, by hand, through its API, or from Selenium, Cypress, Playwright or Puppeteer (as of 7 Nov 2026). It has two systems.

Public Mailinator needs no account: "every possible address @mailinator.com already exists", and anyone can open any inbox (Mailinator FAQs). Its docs are plain about what that means: inboxes and emails "are readable and delete-able by anyone", mail auto-deletes "after a few hours", and attachments aren't allowed (Public Mailbox, as of 7 Nov 2026).

Private domains come with a subscription. You get a domain from Mailinator or use your own, every address on it works the moment mail arrives, and mail stays "until your team's storage is exhausted", when new mail pushes out the oldest (Private Mailbox). Rules on a private domain can click every link in each email, forward it to a webhook, or drop it unsaved for load tests (Private email testing domains). There's also an authenticator for TOTP codes and, on paid plans, real SMS numbers: "$500 per number, per year or $50 per month", with 500 messages a month (SMS Testing, as of 7 Nov 2026).

Either way it only receives: "Mailinator is a receive-only system", so it can't send or reply (FAQs).

For agents, Mailinator has a REST API, SDKs, and a CLI that also starts a local MCP server (mailinator-cli --start-mcp-server); public inboxes need no token, private domains a MAILINATOR_API_KEY (Mailinator MCP). Its temp mail API guide lists an inbox, fetches the message and its links, and says to "Poll every two seconds against a deadline" (Temp Mail API, 5 October 2026). Its own agent guidance says not to send "OTP codes, password reset links, magic links" to public inboxes, and to use private domains instead (Agent Safety and Privacy, as of 7 Nov 2026).

How AgentFirstStack Mail does it

AgentFirstStack Mail is built for an agent doing its own work, not for testing an app's outgoing mail. Your agent gets the account, the inbox and the codes itself:

  1. A key in one request, with no contact details and no verification: POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/signup.
  2. A private inbox in one more: POST https://mail.agentfirststack.com/v1/inboxes {"username":"acme-signups"} gives acme-signups@agentfirstinbox.com, readable only with the project's key or by the people who claimed the project. The address is never given to another account, even after it's deleted, because it may be the recovery email of accounts elsewhere. Plus addresses (acme-signups+github@) give each service its own tag.
  3. The code, from the right sender: GET /v1/inboxes/:id/latest-code?from=github.com&timeout=60 waits on the server, up to 120 seconds, until a message from that domain with a code or verification link arrives. Codes are extracted only with evidence (a "verification code" label, not a price or an order number), links are classed as verify, magic login or password reset, and only mail whose sender domain is authenticated counts, so a stranger who guesses the address can't feed your agent a fake code.
  4. Replies: POST /v1/messages/:id/reply answers in the same thread, and send and forward work too. On the shared domain an inbox replies only to people who wrote to it first; an inbox on your own domain can write to anyone.
  5. Mail reaches a person: forwarding sends each email, or a daily digest, to the people who claimed the project, so the accounts your agent opened don't go unread when its session ends.

Your own domain is free: POST /v1/domains returns the DNS records, and your agent adds them if it can change the DNS (the zone must be on Cloudflare DNS). Signed webhooks, an event feed, threads, search, attachments and a deliverability check of any received message are in the mail manual, and the email inboxes page has the full feature list.

When to choose which

Use Mailinator if

  • You test your own app's email: many test users, thousands of messages a day, a CI run on every build.
  • You need SMS codes from real phone numbers, or TOTP codes from an authenticator.
  • You want every address on a domain to work with no create call, and rules that click each link, post to a webhook or drop mail for load tests.
  • You need team seats, SSO or Enterprise email load testing.
  • You want a throwaway address this minute and privacy doesn't matter: a public inbox needs no signup at all.

Use AgentFirstStack Mail if

  • Your agent signs up for services on a project's behalf, and those accounts will matter later: the inbox should be private, and nobody else should ever be able to take the address.
  • Your agent has to reply or send, not only receive.
  • You want the code only from the sender you expect, with its domain authenticated.
  • The mail should reach a person once your agent stops.
  • You want it with analytics, a tag manager and session replay on one key, or open source you can run yourself.

Moving from Mailinator

  • Nothing is imported. There's no tool to bring Mailinator messages over; they stay in Mailinator until it deletes them.
  • Accounts on public addresses first. If a real account uses a public @mailinator.com address, anyone who knows the address can read its mail, password resets included. Change that account's email to the new inbox.
  • Polling loops become one call. Where code lists an inbox every few seconds and fetches the newest message, latest-code (code and link) or wait (the whole message) blocks until the mail arrives.
  • Unauthenticated test senders. latest-code skips mail whose sender domain isn't authenticated. If a staging server sends without SPF or DKIM, set those up, or pass allow_unauthenticated=true for that test only.
  • Your own domain. Receiving here means pointing the domain's MX records at Cloudflare Email Routing, so it stops receiving at Mailinator. A new subdomain avoids that.
  • High-volume test suites are better left on Mailinator: a project here receives 100 messages a day.

FAQ

Are Mailinator inboxes private?

Public ones aren't. Mailinator's docs say every inbox at @mailinator.com is readable and deletable by anyone, and its mail is deleted after a few hours. Inboxes on a Mailinator private domain, from the free Verified Pro plan or a paid plan, are private to your team.

Can Mailinator send or reply to email?

No. Mailinator's FAQ says it is a receive-only system. AgentFirstStack Mail can send, reply and forward: inboxes on the shared @agentfirstinbox.com domain reply only to people who wrote first, and an inbox on your own domain can write to anyone, within 20 recipients a day per project.

How much does Mailinator cost?

As of 7 November 2026, public inboxes are free, Verified Pro is free after verification, Business is $79 a month billed yearly or $99 month to month, Business Plus is $159 or $199, and Enterprise starts at $699 a month. SMS numbers cost extra. AgentFirstStack Mail is free with fair-use limits.

Is there a temporary email API an AI agent can use on its own?

Yes. With AgentFirstStack Mail, one request returns an API key and another creates an inbox; ttl_days makes the inbox and its mail expire after that many days. Unlike a public disposable address, nobody else can ever take it over. Mailinator's CLI reads public inboxes without a token; its API for private domains needs a Verified Pro or paid account.

Can an AI agent use Mailinator?

Yes. An agent with a browser can open a public inbox, and Mailinator has an API, SDKs and a CLI that also runs a local MCP server. Its own docs say not to send OTP codes, password reset links or magic links to public inboxes, and recommend a private domain for that.

Does AgentFirstStack Mail receive SMS codes?

No, it receives email only. Mailinator rents SMS numbers on paid plans, so it's the better choice when a flow sends its codes by text message.
