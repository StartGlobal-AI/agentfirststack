Web analytics your AI agent sets up

AgentFirstStack Analytics is cookieless web analytics: where visitors come from (AI assistants included), what they do, which goals they reach and what they pay, with funnels, MRR, AI crawler reports and Core Web Vitals. It replaces Google Analytics, Plausible, DataFast and Fathom, it's free on the hosted service, and it's open source (AGPL-3.0, the tracker MIT). An AI agent sets it up alone: one request for a key, a site, the snippet served from your own domain, a goal for each conversion, history from your old tool, then a test it runs itself. You claim the dashboard with a link.

Until it's active, or if you can't, use the path proxy from the install answer, with the proxy key kept server-side. Put the snippet in the head of every page. 5. Create a goal for each conversion and main action, keeping the old tool's event names. Send what only the backend knows (a paid order, a verified sign-up) as server events, with afsa.visitorId() passed through. If we take payments, connect the provider, and ask me for the key you need. 6. If the site ran another analytics tool, import its daily history as the manual's "Move from another tool" describes, up to today, so imports/compare can check our numbers against it. 7. Tell me what you set up, what verify and the setup check returned, what's waiting on me, and give me the claim link.

What your agent does

Every step is a REST call on https://analytics.agentfirststack.com (or the platform host where noted) and an MCP tool on https://agentfirststack.com/mcp. Every create call is safe to repeat: it returns the existing thing.

  1. Gets a key. POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/signup returns one key for every service, with no email or verification. It goes in .env as AGENTFIRSTSTACK_API_KEY.
  2. Creates the site. POST /v1/sites {"domain":"example.com"} (analytics_create_site) returns the site id and install code for HTML, Next.js, Astro, React and WordPress, led by the first-party options.
  3. Serves it from your domain. POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/first-party-domains {"hostname":"edge.example.com"} (afs_add_first_party_domain) gives one CNAME to fp.agentfirststack.com. Once it's active, the snippet loads from https://edge.example.com/_a/s.js, with no server code, so ad blockers that block analytics hosts don't drop visitors. Where DNS can't change, a path proxy on the site does the same.
  4. Adds goals. POST /v1/sites/:id/goals (analytics_create_goal): event goals for clicks and forms, pageview goals for thank-you pages, scroll goals for content. Goals apply to past data too.
  5. Sends what the browser can't see. POST /v1/sites/:id/events (analytics_track_events) for server events, with a dry run. Payments come from Stripe in one call with a restricted key (POST /v1/sites/:id/integrations/stripe/connect, 90 days of history), from Polar, Paddle and LemonSqueezy by webhook, or from POST /v1/sites/:id/payments.
  6. Brings history over. POST /v1/sites/:id/imports (analytics_import_csv) takes a GA4 report, a Plausible report or CSV, or rows from any tool. GET /v1/sites/:id/imports/compare (analytics_compare_import) then checks our daily visitors against the old tool's.
  7. Reports AI crawlers. Bots don't run the tracker, so the site's server or edge code posts their requests to POST /v1/sites/:id/crawls, fire-and-forget, filtered with the prefilter from GET /v1/crawlers.
  8. Sets up your reports. An email destination, a weekly report and alerts for a traffic drop and for no data (/destinations, /reports, /alerts). They reach you once you claim the project.
  9. Tests its own work. POST /v1/sites/:id/verify (analytics_verify_install) fetches the live page and checks the snippet, the script, the Content-Security-Policy and the events. A visit with ?afsa_debug=1 is test traffic it can watch in realtime without touching your reports. Then POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup/test (afs_test_setup) and GET https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup (afs_check_setup).
  10. Hands over. POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/claim-link (afs_create_claim_link) gives you a single-use link, valid for 7 days. You sign in with Google and see the dashboard.

What you get

  • Tracking: a 2.8 KB (gzip) tracker; no cookies by default; pageviews in single-page and hash-routed apps; custom events and props, also from HTML attributes; outbound clicks and downloads; forms and 404s on request; scroll depth, engaged time and Core Web Vitals; site search; UTM tags and ad click ids; consent gating; cross-domain linking.
  • Reports: stats, breakdowns by up to three dimensions, timeseries and comparisons, custom metrics, channel groups, realtime with a live WebSocket stream and a map, top movers, insights, CSV, and one rollup across your sites. Countries, regions and cities come from the edge.
  • Goals and funnels: event, pageview and scroll goals with values, funnels in order or any order, within a visit or up to 30 days, with revenue per step, and saved segments.
  • People: visitors, sessions linked to replay recordings, journeys to a goal, first touch, retention cohorts, path exploration and six attribution models.
  • Revenue and MRR: payments joined to the visit that brought them; MRR, ARR, churn and trial conversion by channel, campaign, country or plan; ad spend for ROAS, CPA and CAC. More on the revenue analytics page.
  • AI search: which AI crawlers read which pages (reported by your server), which assistants send visitors and what those visitors do, the pages read but never visited, and next steps. It doesn't run prompts through ChatGPT or Claude to measure brand mentions. More on the AI search page.
  • Page speed: Core Web Vitals from real visitors at the 75th percentile, by page, device, country and browser, slowest pages first, plus a PageSpeed Insights lab run on demand. More on the page speed page.
  • Data out: raw events as NDJSON, CSV or JSON, read-only SQL with cost limits, and a schema call that lists every event and property.
  • For you: the dashboard, public share links with a password, an expiry or a saved segment, and alerts and weekly or monthly reports by email, Slack or webhook.
  • Privacy: erase one visitor, purge by filter with a dry run, email and phone redaction, path rules, exclusions, a referrer spam filter, Do Not Track and GPC, and attack protection that quarantines floods instead of counting them.

Every endpoint, field and MCP tool is in the analytics manual, and the features page lists the other services.

What the setup check verifies

GET https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup?services=analytics (afs_check_setup) reads what the service has received and returns "ready": true or a fix for each row. For analytics it checks:

CheckWhat it means
analytics.siteAnalytics has a site for the website
analytics.first_partyBrowser events arrive first-party (through the site's own domain), so ad blockers don't drop them
analytics.receivingAnalytics is receiving events from the pages
analytics.domain_verifiedThe site's domain is verified
analytics.proxy_keyEvents through a first-party proxy carry its proxy key
analytics.goalsThere is a goal for each conversion and main action
analytics.goals_countedEvery goal has counted at least once (test traffic included, last 30 days)
analytics.server_eventsConversions only the backend knows arrive as server events (a warning only: not every site has them)
analytics.crawlersAI crawler and search bot visits are reported from the site's server
analytics.owner_emailThe owner gets email from analytics
analytics.weekly_reportA weekly report goes to the owner
analytics.alertsAlerts fire on a traffic drop and when tracking stops

When the project also has a tag container, whose scan finds the tools a site already loads, two more rows follow a move from another tool: migrate.tools (what the site used before is brought over) and migrate.compare (our daily visitors match the old tool's, for the days both ran). The setup test (POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup/test) runs analytics.test_event: a test event reaches analytics. The check fetches nothing from the site, so the agent deploys and makes its test visit first.

Limits

The hosted service is free at any size within the fair-use limits, the same for every project:

  • 10 analytics sites per project, 3 before a person claims it.
  • Up to 400 days of history: raw events older than the site's retention_days are deleted daily.
  • Daily event budgets per site and per account, with a separate budget for server events and payments, so a flood of browser events never refuses what your backend sends. One network's events past its daily share are quarantined, not counted.
  • 300 API reads a minute per project on each service.

GET https://agentfirststack.com/v1/usage shows every limit with what the project has used. Need more? Run your own copy: pnpm afs init <name> --domain example.com --account <id>, then pnpm afs deploy <name>.

When it isn't the right fit

  • Long history. The hosted service keeps up to 400 days of raw events. Imported history stays as daily totals, but year-over-year detail beyond that needs a self-hosted copy with a longer retention.
  • Mobile apps. There's no SDK for iOS or Android. The tracker is for websites; backends send server events.
  • Anonymous visitors across days. Cookieless ids rotate daily, so returning visitors and multi-day attribution work for identified users (a user_id from your server) or in persistent mode, not for anonymous visitors.
  • The Google ecosystem. There are no Google Ads audiences, BigQuery export or Search Console integration. Our tag manager can keep sending conversions to GA4 and Google Ads if you need them.
  • Data-driven attribution. The six models are rule-based; data-driven models aren't offered.
  • A dashboard as the main tool. The dashboard shows traffic, goals, visitors, AI search and page speed. MRR, attribution models and retention cohorts are in the API and MCP, for your agent to read and summarise.
  • EU-only hosting. The hosted service runs on Cloudflare and doesn't offer EU-only data storage.

FAQ

Does AgentFirstStack Analytics use cookies?

No. By default a visitor is a hash of a daily salt, the site, the IP address and the user agent, the salt rotates every day, and nothing is stored on the device. An opt-in persistent mode keeps a random id in localStorage; the tracker can wait for consent with data-require-consent.

What does my agent need from me?

Usually only a few things: the DNS record for the first-party domain if it can't change your DNS, a deploy if it can't deploy, a payment provider key if you take payments, and your click on the claim link. Everything else, from the key to the goals and the tests, it does alone.

Can it bring over my Google Analytics or Plausible history?

Yes, as daily totals and breakdowns by page, source, country and device: a GA4 Data API report or CSV export, a Plausible Stats API report or CSV export, or rows from any other tool. Then imports/compare checks our daily visitors against the old tool's for the days both ran.

Is it really free?

The hosted service is free at any size within the fair-use limits, such as 10 analytics sites per project and up to 400 days of history. The code is open source under AGPL-3.0, with the tracker under MIT, and you can run your own copy on Cloudflare with your own limits.

How do I see the numbers?

Open the claim link your agent gives you and sign in with Google to see the dashboard, or ask your agent, which reads every report over REST and MCP. Your agent can also make a public share link with a password, an expiry or a saved segment.
