AI visibility trackers vs AI search analytics: two ways to measure LLM visibility

An AI visibility tracker such as Profound, Peec AI, Otterly or Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit runs prompts through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and other assistants every day and scores how often the answers mention and cite your brand, on paid plans from $29 to $495 a month (as of 7 November 2026). AgentFirstStack doesn't do that. Its free AI search analytics, which an AI agent sets up, measures what AI crawlers read on your site, what the visitors assistants send do, and what to change. To know what assistants say about you, use a tracker; the two work well together.

Updated 7 November 2026

What AI visibility trackers do

A tracker starts from prompts: the questions your buyers might ask an assistant, such as "best invoicing tool for freelancers". You choose them, or the tool suggests them. It sends them to the assistants your plan covers on a schedule, keeps the answers and scores them: whether your brand is named, where, in what tone, against which competitors, and which pages the answer cites. In the vendors' own words, as of 7 Nov 2026:

  • Profound runs structured prompts, "analyzing where and how your brand appears in responses", and tracks citations, sentiment, ranking and competitive presence (pricing FAQ).
  • Peec AI reports "Your visibility, position, sentiment, and share of voice, tracked daily against competitors", plus the sources answers cite (pricing).
  • Otterly describes its AI Search Analytics as a way to "Track brand mentions and citations across AI engines" (pricing).
  • Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit runs common queries "through LLMs like ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode" and analyzes how your brand is mentioned (knowledge base).

That's what people mean by LLM visibility: how present a brand is in the answers language models give. A tracker's numbers are a sample of it: the prompts you picked, run in the assistants, countries and languages your plan includes. Answers can name you without anyone clicking through to your site, and only a tracker sees those.

Trackers have started measuring the site side too. Profound's Agent Analytics says it tracks "when, how often, and which AI bots access your content", from server logs rather than JavaScript, and "Differentiates real AI bots from spoofed crawlers" (Agent Analytics). Peec AI's pricing page lists crawl insights ("Which AI bots hit which pages, how often"), a robots.txt crawlability audit and AI referrals on every plan. Otterly includes Agent Analytics on its Standard and Premium plans. Semrush's toolkit has a site audit that looks for issues that might stop AI bots crawling your content. Their crawler data comes through integrations with your CDN or host (Cloudflare, Vercel, WordPress and others) or uploaded log files.

What AgentFirstStack measures instead

AgentFirstStack's AI search is part of its web analytics, and it measures what happens on your side:

  • What AI crawlers read. Your server reports bot requests (POST /v1/sites/:id/crawls, only for user agents matching the prefilter from GET /v1/crawlers), and each is classified: AI answers (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User), AI search (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot), AI training (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot) and search engines. You see which pages each reads, which got errors, and what they asked for of robots.txt, llms.txt and the sitemap.
  • The visitors assistants send. Visits referred by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and others, where they land, and what they do next to every other channel: engagement, conversions, conversion rate and revenue, with your own goals and payments.
  • What to change. GET /v1/sites/:id/ai-search (MCP get_ai_search) ends with next steps: a robots.txt that keeps out a search or answer bot (checked against the site's own robots.txt), pages that fail for crawlers, a missing llms.txt, pages bots read that send no one.

It's free on the hosted service within fair-use limits, open source (AGPL-3.0, browser scripts MIT) and self-hostable on Cloudflare. Your agent sets it up with one request for a key and reads it over REST or MCP. What it doesn't do: run prompts, count mentions or citations in answers, score sentiment, or compare you with competitors. It classifies crawlers by user agent without checking their IPs, and it can't see an answer that names you if nobody clicks.

Why the two work together

A tracker tells you where you're missing from answers. AgentFirstStack tells you whether the bots behind those answers can read your pages, and whether the visits that follow are worth having. A typical loop:

  1. The tracker shows prompts where competitors are cited and you aren't, and which of their pages get cited.
  2. The AI search report shows whether OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot and the fetchers that answer in real time read your page on that topic, got an error, or stopped at robots.txt.
  3. You fix access, and rewrite the page so it answers the question in its first paragraph, with specifics worth quoting.
  4. The report shows whether the bots read it again and whether visitors from ChatGPT or Perplexity arrive and convert; the tracker shows whether the answers changed.

When to choose which

Use an AI visibility tracker if

  • You need to know what assistants say about your brand, product or category, and how you compare with competitors there.
  • Your team reports share of voice, sentiment or citation share in AI answers.
  • You want to know which questions to write for: Semrush has prompt research, and Profound lists prompt volume searches.
  • Few visitors arrive from assistants yet, and you want to know whether you're named in answers anyway.
  • You want crawler data checked against bots' real IPs: Profound says its Agent Analytics does that.

Use AgentFirstStack AI search if

  • You want to know whether AI crawlers can and do read your pages, and fix what stops them.
  • You want visitors from assistants measured against your other channels on conversions and revenue, in the analytics you already use for everything else.
  • You want it free and open source, set up and read by your agent over an API.

Use both if

  • You're putting real work into AI search: the tracker measures the answers, AgentFirstStack measures the site and the visits.

Running both

There's nothing to move. AgentFirstStack doesn't import tracker data, and it doesn't need to: the tracker keeps running its prompts, and AgentFirstStack starts counting crawls and visits from the day your agent sets it up. If a tracker already gets your crawler logs through a CDN or host integration, you can keep it; AgentFirstStack's crawler reporting is separate code in your own server or middleware.

FAQ

What is LLM visibility?

How often, where and how a brand shows up in the answers that assistants built on large language models give: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode. AI visibility trackers measure it by running prompts and scoring the answers for mentions, position, sentiment and the sources cited.

Does AgentFirstStack track brand mentions in ChatGPT or Perplexity?

No. It doesn't run prompts through any assistant, so it can't say how often answers mention or cite you, or how you compare with competitors there. It measures your own site: which AI crawlers read which pages, which assistants send visitors and how those visitors convert, and what to change.

How much does an AI visibility tracker cost?

As of 7 November 2026: Otterly from $29 a month for 15 prompts, Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit $99 a month for 25 prompts, Peec AI from $95 a month for 50 prompts, and Profound a free 7-day trial of 50 prompts with Enterprise priced on request. Higher plans run to $489 (Otterly) and $495 (Peec AI) a month.

Do AI visibility trackers also show AI crawlers?

Several do. Profound's Agent Analytics reads server or CDN logs, Peec AI lists crawl insights, a robots.txt audit and AI referrals on its pricing page, and Otterly includes Agent Analytics from its Standard plan. Semrush's toolkit has a site audit for AI crawler access rather than crawler logs.

Can I see visitors from ChatGPT in Google Analytics?

Yes. GA4's default channel group now has an AI Assistant channel for sources such as ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Copilot and Grok (Google's help page, as of 7 Nov 2026). GA4 doesn't show crawlers: it excludes known bots and doesn't report how many it excluded (known bot-traffic exclusion).

Can AgentFirstStack import data from my AI visibility tracker?

No. There's no import for tracker data, and there's nothing to move: the tracker keeps measuring answers, and AgentFirstStack starts measuring your site and its visitors from the day it's set up.
