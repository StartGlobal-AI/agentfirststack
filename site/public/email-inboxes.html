Email inboxes your AI agent creates itself

AgentFirstStack Mail gives your agent email inboxes of its own: one request creates <name>@agentfirstinbox.com with no DNS, or an inbox on the project's own domain. Your agent uses the address to sign up for the services a project needs, waits on the server for the verification email, and gets the code or link back, only from the sender it named. It reads threads and attachments, replies, and forwards the mail to you once you claim the project. It replaces a Gmail account per project, AgentMail or Mailinator, is free on the hosted service and open source (AGPL-3.0), and your agent sets it up and tests it.

Why your agent needs its own inbox

Setting up a project means signing up for things: a payment provider, an email sender, a developer tool. Each wants an email address, and most send a code or a link before the account works. If your agent uses your personal address, you read every code to it, and every password reset for those accounts lands in your mailbox. If it uses a public disposable inbox, anyone who guesses the name can read the code, and later the resets.

An inbox of its own lets your agent finish each signup alone. The address stays with your project: an address on agentfirstinbox.com is never given to another account, even after it's deleted, because it may be the recovery email of accounts elsewhere. And since nobody reads the inbox once your agent's session ends, forwarding sends its mail on to you, so the receipts, notices and resets of those accounts don't go unread.

What your agent does

  1. Gets a key with POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/signup, or reuses the one in .env. The same key works for analytics, tags and replay.
  2. Creates the inbox with POST https://mail.agentfirststack.com/v1/inboxes (MCP create_inbox, or mail_create_inbox on the all-services server). Creating the same address again returns it with "existing": true.
  3. On your own domain, first POST /v1/domains, which returns every DNS record needed: an ownership TXT record, then the records for receiving and sending. It adds them if it can change the DNS, otherwise it gives them to you, then runs POST /v1/domains/:domain/verify, which checks each record live and says what to fix. A send-only domain ("receiving": false) keeps its existing MX records, such as Google Workspace.
  4. Signs up for a service with a plus address (acme-signups+github@agentfirstinbox.com), so each service's mail carries its own tag.
  5. Waits for the code with GET /v1/inboxes/:id/latest-code?from=github.com (MCP get_latest_code), which blocks until the email arrives and returns the code and link.
  6. Sets forwarding the way you chose: PATCH /v1/inboxes/:id with {"forward":{"mode":"each"}} or {"forward":{"mode":"digest","digest_hour":8,"timezone":"Europe/Berlin"}}.
  7. Tests it with the setup test and the setup check (below), and gives you a claim link (POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/claim-link) so you see the inbox in a dashboard.
M=https://mail.agentfirststack.com
H="Authorization: Bearer $AGENTFIRSTSTACK_API_KEY"

# An inbox, forwarded to the owner as each email arrives
curl -s -X POST $M/v1/inboxes -H "$H" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"username":"acme-signups","forward":{"mode":"each"}}'
# -> { "inbox": { "id": "inb_...", "address": "acme-signups@agentfirstinbox.com", "forward": { ... } }, ... }

# Sign up at github.com with acme-signups+github@agentfirstinbox.com, then:
curl -s "$M/v1/inboxes/$INBOX/latest-code?from=github.com&tag=github&timeout=60" -H "$H"
# -> { "sender_domain": "github.com", "code": "482913", "link": "https://...", "link_kind": "verify", "auth_verdict": "pass", ... }

What you get

  • Inboxes: on agentfirstinbox.com in one request, or on your own domain. Plus addresses (name+tag@) land in name@ with the tag on each message, and every list and wait call filters by it. ttl_days makes an inbox and its mail expire. On your own domain, a catch-all inbox can take mail for addresses that don't exist.
  • Waiting for a code or link: wait and latest-code hold the request on the server until mail arrives, up to 120 seconds, and count mail from up to 60 seconds before the call, so nothing that landed in between is missed. from is the sender's domain or address, never a display name. Only mail whose sender domain is authenticated counts, and without from, codes from two sender domains answer 409 ambiguous_sender instead of guessing.
  • Extraction: one-time codes only with evidence (a word like "verification code" next to them; prices, dates, phone and order numbers are left out), links classed as verify, magic login, password reset or unsubscribe, SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, calendar invites, and reply_text, the reply without the quoted history.
  • Reading: messages with filters (unread, sender, tag, has a code, authenticated or not, labels), threads, search across inboxes, the raw email, attachments, and labels on messages and threads. Turning a PDF or Word attachment into text needs a Workers AI binding: it's off on the hosted service today and available when you self-host.
  • Sending: send, reply (in the same thread) and forward, with up to 50 recipients and 32 attachments, safe to retry with an Idempotency-Key, scheduled up to 7 days ahead, and delivery status per recipient: delivered, bounced, rejected or marked as spam.
  • Forwarding to you: each email as it arrives, or one digest a day at the hour and time zone you pick, to everyone who claimed the project with Google sign-in. It starts when you claim the project, with one catch-up digest of what arrived before. Mail from senders that aren't authenticated, bounces and auto-replies are never forwarded; you can still read them.
  • Events: signed webhooks for received, sent, delivered, bounced, complained, failed and forwarded mail and verified domains (HMAC-SHA256 and Standard Webhooks headers, 8 attempts), and an event feed (GET /v1/events) your agent can wait on without a public URL, kept 7 days.
  • Deliverability report: send your own site's email to an inbox and GET /v1/messages/:id/analysis checks it: aligned SPF, DKIM and DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, a text part, size and Gmail's 102 KB clipping, images without alt text, scripts and forms, link domains against the From domain, URL shorteners and plain http links. There's no spam score.
  • Safety: text a third party wrote is marked untrusted in REST answers and fenced in MCP results, so your agent treats it as data. Allow and block lists refuse senders before the message is read, and sending pauses by itself if too many recipients bounce or report spam.
  • MCP: 46 mail tools at https://mail.agentfirststack.com/mcp, or with the mail_ prefix on the all-services server at https://agentfirststack.com/mcp.

What the setup check verifies

The setup check (GET https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup, MCP afs_check_setup; add ?services=mail to check mail only) reads what's configured. The setup test (POST https://agentfirststack.com/v1/setup/test, MCP afs_test_setup) sends a real email.

Limits

On the hosted service, per project; the number in brackets applies until a person claims it. All of them are on the limits page and in the mail manual, and GET /v1/usage shows what the project has used.

  • 10 inboxes (5).
  • 20 recipients sent a day (10), counted across all the project's inboxes, your own domain included. Inboxes on agentfirstinbox.com reply only to people who wrote first.
  • Mail on agentfirstinbox.com addresses is deleted after 14 days. An inbox holds 100 messages (50); when it's full, the oldest mail of the sender that holds the most is removed to make room.
  • 100 received messages a day (50), and 20 a day from one sender to one inbox (10).
  • 1 GiB of stored mail (100 MiB), and messages up to 25 MiB (10 MiB).
  • 3 waits at once (2), 2 per inbox.
  • 3 webhooks (2), and 5,000 events a day in the event feed (1,000).
  • 100 forwarded emails a day; past that, the rest goes into one digest the next day.

When it isn't the right fit

  • Bulk, cold or marketing email. Shared-domain inboxes are reply-only and a project sends to 20 recipients a day. Use a sending service built for volume.
  • Many inboxes, such as one per customer. A project has 10; AgentMail's paid plans have more.
  • Testing your own app's email at volume. A project receives 100 messages a day. Mailinator is built for QA and CI testing at that scale.
  • SMS or authenticator codes. It receives email only.
  • A long-term mailbox for people. There's no IMAP, SMTP or drafts, and shared-domain mail is kept 14 days. People read the forwarded copies in their own mailbox.
  • A domain whose DNS isn't on Cloudflare. Your own domain needs its zone on Cloudflare DNS, because receiving and sending run on Cloudflare Email Routing and Email Sending.
  • A service that refuses the address. If a signup form won't take an @agentfirstinbox.com address, an inbox on the project's own domain is the way round.

FAQ

Why does an AI agent need its own email inbox?

Setting up a project means signing up for services, and most of them send a verification code or link before the account works. With its own inbox, your agent finishes those signups without asking you for each code, and the accounts' mail stays out of your personal mailbox. Forwarding sends it on to you, as each email arrives or in a daily digest, once you claim the project.

Is AgentFirstStack Mail free?

Yes. The hosted service is free with fair-use limits: 10 inboxes per project, 20 sent recipients a day, and 14-day retention on the shared domain. It's open source under AGPL-3.0, so you can self-host it on Cloudflare with your own limits.

How does a verification code reach my agent?

Your agent calls latest-code with the inbox and the service's domain, for example from=stripe.com. The call waits on the server, up to 120 seconds, until a message from that domain with a code or verification link arrives, and returns both already extracted. By default it only counts mail whose sender domain is authenticated, so a forged email can't feed your agent a code.

Can the inbox send email to anyone?

Not from the shared @agentfirstinbox.com domain. Those inboxes reply only to people who emailed them first from an authenticated address, plus the project's own inboxes and the people who claimed it. An inbox on your own verified domain can write to anyone, within the project's 20 recipients a day.

How long is mail kept?

On the hosted service, mail on @agentfirstinbox.com addresses is deleted after 14 days, and an inbox holds up to 100 messages. Mail on your own domain isn't deleted after 14 days. Your agent can also give an inbox ttl_days, so the inbox and its mail are deleted sooner.

Can it receive SMS codes?

No. AgentFirstStack Mail receives email only. A service that sends its codes only by text message needs a phone number.
