Microsoft Clarity alternative: session replay your AI agent sets up and reads

Microsoft Clarity is free session replay and heatmaps with no traffic limits, recordings kept 30 days and mobile app SDKs (as of 7 November 2026), built for people in its dashboard; an AI agent gets a local MCP server and a Data Export API capped at 10 requests a project a day. AgentFirstStack Replay is free, open-source replay your agent sets up and reads itself: strict masking in the browser, no cookies, consent where required, and each session as a transcript over REST and MCP. Stay with Clarity for every session at any traffic, mobile apps or longer retention.

Updated 7 November 2026

What Microsoft Clarity is

Microsoft Clarity is a behavior analytics tool for websites and apps: session recordings, heatmaps and insights such as rage clicks, dead clicks, excessive scrolling, quick backs and JavaScript errors (semantic metrics). Its FAQ says "Clarity is a free service forever" and "You never encounter traffic limits or are compelled to upgrade to a paid version", with "No sampling" and no limit on the number of projects (Clarity FAQ, as of 7 Nov 2026). Funnels and smart events are set up without code.

It also does things for people that AgentFirstStack doesn't: Copilot answers questions about the dashboard and summarizes heatmaps; a Google Analytics integration adds a Clarity dimension to one web property; and AI Visibility shows "how often your website appears in AI-generated answers", next to Bot Activity, which needs a connection to a CDN such as Cloudflare, Fastly or Amazon CloudFront (same FAQ). Our AI search reports AI crawler reads and visitors sent by assistants, but doesn't track citations in answers.

How AgentFirstStack Replay does it

The difference is who sets it up and who reads it. There's no project to create in a dashboard: your agent makes the account, the site and the install, checks it, and reads what visitors did.

  1. Setup by the agent. POST /v1/signup returns a key, POST https://replay.agentfirststack.com/v1/sites creates the site, and verify fetches the live homepage to check the install, the Content-Security-Policy and the domain. Linked to AgentFirstStack analytics, the tracker loads the recorder: one snippet for both.
  2. Strict masking, no cookies. Every text node and typed value is masked in the browser before it's sent; your agent unmasks interface text only. Password, payment-card and one-time-code fields can never be unmasked. The session lives in the tab's sessionStorage, and the IP address is never stored.
  3. Consent that waits. With require_consent: "eea", visitors from the EEA, the UK and Switzerland aren't recorded at all until they consent, through afsr('consent', true), Google Consent Mode, an IAB TCF v2 CMP or the AgentFirstStack consent banner. Everyone else is recorded at once. That's a different choice from Clarity's, which keeps recording without consent, one pageview per session.
  4. Sessions as text. Each session is a transcript of pages, labelled clicks, masked inputs, scroll depth, errors and the signals (rage, dead and error clicks, quick backs, excessive scrolling, failed requests), plus summaries across up to 1,000 sessions, heatmaps as data, grouped errors, funnel drop-offs and survey answers. API reads are limited to 300 a minute per project, not 10 a day.
  5. Signals go where you report. Rage clicks, JS errors and the rest arrive in AgentFirstStack analytics as events joined to the visitor, and in a tags container as server events if you link one.
  6. Served from your own domain. A first-party domain (one CNAME) serves replay at /_r/* with analytics and tags, so the recorder doesn't load from a third-party host, which is what ad blockers block.

The hosted service records 200 sessions per site a day (100 until a person claims the project), each up to 30 minutes, and keeps them 14 days. Everything else is on the session replay page and in the replay manual.

When to choose which

Stay with Microsoft Clarity if

  • You need every session recorded at high traffic, free: Clarity has no traffic limits and no sampling, and our hosted service stops at 200 sessions per site a day.
  • You need recordings for more than 14 days, or heatmap history for months.
  • You record native or hybrid mobile apps.
  • People on your team watch recordings and read heatmaps in a dashboard, and like Copilot, smart events, the Google Analytics integration or Shopify purchase insights.
  • You want reports on citations in AI answers, which our AI search doesn't do.

Use AgentFirstStack Replay if

  • An agent should set replay up, test it and report back, without a person creating a project or a token.
  • The agent needs to read many sessions as text, search them by signal or survey answer, and group what went wrong, without a 10-request daily quota.
  • You want every text masked by default, no cookies, nothing recorded from EEA, UK or Swiss visitors before consent, and deletion of one visitor's data.
  • You want replay with analytics, tags and inboxes on one key, open source and self-hostable.

You can run both while you compare; each adds its own recorder to the page.

Moving from Clarity

  • Recordings don't move. Clarity's FAQ says you can't download specific recordings, and there's no import. Keep Clarity until you no longer need its 30 days of recordings.
  • Masking rules do. Elements marked data-clarity-mask keep working when [data-clarity-mask] is in the replay site's mask_selectors; selectors from Clarity's mask-by-element list go there too. Review unmask rules before copying them: our default is stricter, and unmask_selectors never reveal typed values.
  • Consent follows the same source. If Clarity gets consent from Google Consent Mode, set consent_source: "gcm"; with an IAB TCF platform, "tcf"; with your own banner, call afsr('consent', true) where it calls clarity('consentv2', ...).
  • The setup check knows. When the tags scan finds Clarity on the site, the setup check's "other tools" row lists it as staying until its recordings expire. Your agent removes Clarity's code only when you say so.

FAQ

Is Microsoft Clarity really free?

Yes. As of 7 November 2026, Microsoft's Clarity FAQ says it is a free service forever, with no traffic limits, no sampling and no paid version to upgrade to. AgentFirstStack Replay's hosted service is free too, but with fair-use limits: 200 recorded sessions per site a day.

What does Clarity do better than AgentFirstStack Replay?

Volume, retention and people. Clarity records every session at any traffic, keeps recordings 30 days and heatmap data 9 months, has SDKs for Android, iOS, React Native and Flutter apps, and has a dashboard with Copilot chat, funnels, a Google Analytics integration and AI Visibility reports for people who work in it.

Does Clarity need cookie consent in Europe?

Clarity has enforced consent signals for visits from the EEA, the UK and Switzerland since 31 October 2025. Without consent it sets no cookies and treats each pageview as a separate session, so journeys, funnels and quick backs lose accuracy. AgentFirstStack Replay with require_consent set to eea records nothing from those visitors until they consent, and never sets cookies.

Can an AI agent read Clarity recordings?

It can list them. Clarity's MCP server runs locally with a Data Export API token and lists recordings by filter, which you watch in Clarity, and the Data Export API allows 10 requests per project a day over the last 1 to 3 days. AgentFirstStack Replay returns each session as a text transcript, with summaries, heatmaps, errors and funnels over REST and MCP.

Can I move my Clarity recordings to AgentFirstStack?

No. Clarity's FAQ says you can't download specific recordings, and AgentFirstStack can't import them. Keep Clarity installed until you no longer need its history, and copy its masking rules into the replay site's selectors.

How do I delete one visitor's data?

In Clarity, per its FAQ, you delete the entire project. In AgentFirstStack Replay, delete_visitor_data removes one person's sessions and survey answers by user id, analytics visitor id, session ids or email, and get_visitor shows what is stored about them first.
